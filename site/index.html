AI support platform · Case study

Hundreds of emails. One queue that triages itself.

Support teams do not drown in answers. They drown in triage: reading every message, picking a category, deciding who owns it, and writing a reply from scratch. SolutionDesk turns inbound email into tickets, classifies each request with Google Gemini, answers from an approved knowledge base retrieved over pgvector, and escalates anything it cannot support safely.

Project features

A complete desk, not a single AI endpoint.

SolutionDesk is a ticket management system with AI woven through it. Admins manage the roster; agents work the queue with AI assistance; the automated path answers only what the knowledge base supports.

Inbox to ticket

The SendGrid inbound webhook parses sender, subject, and body, then creates a ticket. Replies with the same sender and subject are appended to the existing open ticket instead of creating duplicates.

AI classification

A background job assigns exactly one category: general question, technical question, or refund request. Invalid model output is rejected rather than saved.

Retrieval-grounded answers

The knowledge base is embedded into Postgres with pgvector. The model receives only the top-k chunks that pass a minimum similarity score — never the whole file.

Auto-resolve or escalate

A supported ticket gets a warm reply, an outbound email, and a resolved status. An unsupported or sensitive ticket stays open for a human, without a wasted model call.

Agent workspace

Ticket list with status and category filters, sorting, a full conversation view, AI summaries, and AI-polished replies that an agent approves before sending.

Admin and roles

Admins create, edit, and remove agent accounts. Agents see the queue and respond. Sign-up is disabled; accounts are seeded or created by an admin.

Dashboard analytics

Open, resolved, and closed counts, tickets by category, daily volume, average resolve time, and the AI resolution rate — with quick filters into the ticket list.

Audit trail

Every AI decision is recorded with the model, retrieved chunk IDs, best score, and outcome, so routing and quality can be reviewed after the fact.

Operational safety

Queue retries, Sentry error reporting, and a worker that returns a failed ticket to open instead of leaving it stranded in processing.

The pain point

Slow triage is the real support bottleneck.

The team receives hundreds of support emails a day. Every message has to be opened, read, understood, classified, and answered. Most of that work is repetitive, but the queue does not distinguish between a password question and a refund dispute, so agents pay the same attention cost for both.

The result is predictable. First responses are slow, the pressure to keep up produces impersonal canned replies, and the genuinely urgent tickets compete for attention with routine ones. There is also no audit trail explaining why a message was routed or answered a certain way, which makes the process hard to improve and risky to automate.

Time lost to triage

Agents spend their day reading and labelling messages instead of solving the cases that actually need a human decision.

Impersonal replies

Under volume pressure, answers become canned. Customers notice, and the support experience feels like a form letter.

Unsafe automation

A chatbot left alone will guess at refunds and account changes. Without grounding and a handoff rule, automating support creates more risk than it removes.

The design goal

Automate the known, repeatable questions end to end, and make the handoff to a human the default for anything sensitive, ambiguous, or unsupported. Reliability over a clever demo.

Implementation details

How it is built, layer by layer.

A React client and an Express API share a PostgreSQL database. Email enters through a webhook; classification and resolution run as background pg-boss jobs; every AI answer is grounded in retrieved knowledge-base chunks and gated by a score threshold.

Monorepo and request path

Structureclient/ (React 18 + Vite) and server/ (Express + TypeScript) are npm workspaces, with shared types in core/.
RoutingVite proxies /api and /health to the server, so the browser works against one origin.
StateAxios with withCredentials for transport; TanStack Query for all server state, caching, and mutations.
2

Data model

UsersA Better Auth user with a custom role field: admin or agent.
TicketsStatus open → resolved → closed, category general_question · technical_question · refund_request, assigned agent, and the full message thread.
KnowledgeKbArticle and KbChunk tables with a vector column and an HNSW cosine index, plus an AiDecision audit table.
EnumsServer code uses Prisma enums for role, status, and category — never hardcoded strings.
3

Email to ticket

InboundSendGrid parses the message; the webhook stores the sender, subject, plain text, and HTML body, and creates a ticket assigned to the AI agent.
ThreadingThe same sender and subject reuse the existing open ticket, so a conversation stays in one place.
AsyncClassification is queued immediately, so receiving mail never waits on a model response.
4

Classification worker

InputThe ticket subject and body.
OutputExactly one of the three categories. Anything else is rejected and reported, not persisted.
RuntimeA pg-boss job with retries. A failure does not block the API process.
5

RAG retrieval

Importknowledge-base.md is split into Q&A articles; each answer is embedded with gemini-embedding-001 and stored in kb_chunk.
QueryThe ticket text is embedded with the same model and compared by cosine distance to find the top-k chunks.
GateOnly chunks above a minimum similarity score are passed to the model. No match means escalation without an LLM call.
Indexpgvector · HNSW · cosine
6

Auto-resolution worker

ContextThe ticket subject and message, the customer's first name, and only the retrieved Q&A chunks.
ContractAnswer only from the approved knowledge base. If it is insufficient, return ESCALATE. Never invent a refund decision, account change, security response, or policy.
OutcomeA safe answer becomes an agent reply, the ticket is marked resolved, and an outbound email is queued. On failure the ticket returns to open.
7

Authentication and roles

SessionsBetter Auth stores sessions in PostgreSQL through the Prisma adapter. No JWTs.
GuardrequireAuth middleware resolves the session and attaches it to the request; protected routes return 401 without one.
ClientA protected-route wrapper redirects unauthenticated visitors to the login page and exposes the session role to the UI.
8

Agent-assist AI

SummaryAn endpoint generates a short summary of a long ticket so an agent can catch up without reading every message.
PolishAn endpoint rewrites an agent's draft into a warmer, clearer reply. The agent still sends it.
ProviderGoogle Gemini through the Vercel AI SDK; the API key lives only in the server environment.
Runtime flow

Customer email in, grounded reply or human handoff out.

The AI and human paths share the same ticket conversation, so an agent can see the original email, the classification, the AI response or escalation, and every previous reply before writing.

Customer-to-response flow
Customer | | sends support email v SolutionDesk receives email | | creates ticket and stores conversation v AI reads the ticket | +--> Classifies category | (general / technical / refund) | +--> Embeds ticket, retrieves KB chunks | +--> A chunk passes the score gate | | | v | AI writes a reply from those chunks | | | v | Outbound email sent to Customer | | | v | Ticket becomes Resolved | +--> No chunk passes, or request is sensitive | v Ticket stays Open | v Human Agent reviews the conversation | v Agent writes and sends the reply

Open

Received and awaiting action. Escalations and worker failures return here so a person can pick them up.

Resolved

Answered and considered handled. The AI sets this only after sending a grounded reply.

Closed

No further action needed. An agent closes a resolved ticket when the thread is finished.

Safety and design decisions

Least autonomy that still removes the busywork.

The interesting engineering is not the prompt. It is the boundary around it: what the model is allowed to see, what it is allowed to say, and when the system refuses to let it answer at all.

  • Grounding is enforced, not requested. The model receives only retrieved chunks, and the chunks come from approved articles. A citation cannot point at a document it never saw.
  • The retrieval gate can refuse. If nothing clears the minimum score, the ticket escalates before a model call. That is cheaper, faster, and safer than asking a model to answer from nothing.
  • Sensitive requests need a human. Exceptions to policy, charge disputes, security or legal concerns, and private account access require review, regardless of what retrieval returns.
  • AI output is never a direct send. The worker writes a ticket reply; a separate outbound job delivers it. Agent-facing summaries and polish always wait for human approval.
  • Failures fail open to a person. A failed AI call returns the ticket to open, queue retries handle transient errors, and Sentry reports the rest.
  • Decisions leave a trace. ai_decision stores the model, chunk IDs, best score, and outcome, so a bad route can be explained later.
  • Credentials stay server-side. Sign-up is disabled, sessions live in PostgreSQL, and the Gemini key never reaches the browser.
Why this shape

A support desk is judged on correctness, not novelty. The system is deliberately narrow: it answers the questions the knowledge base supports and routes everything else. That constraint is what makes it safe to put in front of real customers.

Built with · each layer has a job

A small stack, chosen for the pipeline.

The frontend, API, database, auth, AI, and email layers are separate on purpose. Each one can be replaced without rewriting the ticket model or the agent experience.
